title: "How Many Children?"
---

A mathematician, Professor Smith, is talking with a visitor, Jones, who hears
children playing out back. Smith explains that the kids in the yard come from
four families: his own plus the Browns, the Greens and the Blacks. Smith's
family has the most children, the Browns have fewer, the Greens fewer still, and
the Blacks the fewest. In all there are fewer than 18 children, and the
**product** of the four family sizes equals Smith's house number, which Jones
saw when he arrived.

Jones works on it with pencil and paper, then says he needs one more fact and
asks whether the Blacks have more than one child. Smith answers, and Jones
immediately gives the correct number of children in every family.

Jones knew the house number, so for him the rest was easy. But it turns out the
family sizes can be pinned down using only what is stated above, without the
house number. What are the sizes of the four families?
