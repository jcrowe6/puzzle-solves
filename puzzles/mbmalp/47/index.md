title: "Termite and 27 Cubes"
---

A large cube is built by gluing together 27 small identical wooden cubes (a
$3 \times 3 \times 3$ block). A termite starts at the center of a face of one of
the outer cubes and bores a tunnel that passes through each small cube. It
always moves parallel to an edge of the large cube, never diagonally.

[Figure: a $3\times3\times3$ cube drawn in perspective, with the termite sitting
on the top face near the back right corner.]

Can the termite bore through each of the 26 outer cubes exactly once and then
end its trip by entering the central cube for the first time? Show how, or
prove that it is impossible.

Once inside a small cube the termite stays inside the large cube; it may not
crawl out along the surface to re-enter somewhere else (that would make the
problem trivial).
