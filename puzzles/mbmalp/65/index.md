title: "Reverse the Dog"

Thirteen paper matches form a dog that faces left. The body is a rectangle of
four matches (two long, two short); the head is a small triangle of matches on
the left; two matches make each of the two legs; and a single match makes an
upward-slanting tail on the right.

[Figure: the match dog facing left: a rectangular body (two horizontal matches
on top and two on the bottom, joined by vertical matches at each end), a
triangular head attached at the left end, one angled leg match below each end,
and a tail match slanting up and to the right at the top right corner. Dotted
lines at the right end show alternative positions: a dotted tail position
slanting downward, and a dotted position where the head's bottom match could be
placed.]

Moving the tail down to the top dotted line and the bottom match of the head
to the other dotted line turns the dog around to face right, but the tail
(now on the left) droops downward.

Can you move just **two** matches so the dog faces right with the tail still
pointing upward?
