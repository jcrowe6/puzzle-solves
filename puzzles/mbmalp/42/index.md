title: "The Sliced Cube and the Sliced Doughnut"
---

An engineer with a knack for picturing three-dimensional shapes is having
coffee and doughnuts. Looking at a sugar cube, he reasons: a horizontal plane
through the center cuts a square; a vertical plane through the center and four
of the cube's corners cuts an oblong rectangle. Then he imagines another cut,
and the section he pictures is a **regular hexagon**.

How is that plane positioned? If the cube's edge is half an inch, what is the
hexagon's side?

Next he looks at a doughnut lying flat on a plate. A horizontal plane through
the center gives two concentric circles. A vertical plane through the center
gives two separate circles, apart by the width of the hole. But with the plane
tilted a certain way, the section is two **intersecting** perfect circles.

How is that plane positioned? If the doughnut is a perfect torus with an outer
diameter of 3 inches and a hole 1 inch across, what are the diameters of the
two intersecting circles?
