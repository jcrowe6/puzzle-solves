title: "The Repetitious Number"
---

Here is a parlor trick. Spectator A writes down any three-digit number and then
writes the same three digits again right after it, forming a six-digit number
(for instance, 394 becomes 394,394). With your back turned, you have A hand the
paper to spectator B and ask B to divide the number by 7. You assure B that
there will be no remainder, and there isn't (394,394 ÷ 7 = 56,342).

B passes the result to spectator C, who divides by 11, again with no remainder
(56,342 ÷ 11 = 5,122). Next, spectator D divides that by 13, and once more it
divides evenly (5,122 ÷ 13 = 394). The final quotient is written on a slip,
folded, and given to you. Without looking, you hand it to A and say it contains
A's original three-digit number, and it does.

Prove that the trick works no matter which three digits the first spectator
chooses.
