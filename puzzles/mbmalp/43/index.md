title: "Bisecting Yin and Yang"
---

Two mathematicians dine at a restaurant named Yin and Yang and talk about the
symbol on its menu. One mentions that there is a neat way to draw a single
straight line across the circle that cuts the area of the Yin and the area of
the Yang each exactly in half.

[Figure: the familiar yin-yang symbol: a circle divided by an S-shaped curve into
a dark teardrop-shaped half (bulbous head at the upper left) and a light half
(head at the lower right). It is a plain two-tone circle with no inner dots.]

Take the Yin and Yang to be separated by two semicircles (the S-curve is made
of two semicircular arcs, each with half the main circle's radius). Show how one
straight line can bisect both areas at once.
