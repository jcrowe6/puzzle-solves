title: "Cross the Network"
---

A classic topological puzzle: draw one continuous line across a closed network
of segments so that it crosses every segment exactly once. The network here has
16 segments in total.

[Figure: a wide rectangle cut by a horizontal line running its full width, a bit
above the middle, into a top and bottom band. The top band is divided into three
cells by two vertical segments (one about a third of the way across, one about
two-thirds), each running from the top edge down to the horizontal line. The
bottom band is divided into two cells by a single vertical segment, roughly
midway across, running from the horizontal line to the bottom edge. Counting
each stretch between junctions or corners as one segment gives 16: 3 along the
top edge, 2 along the bottom edge, 2 on each short side, 4 along the horizontal
line, and 3 vertical dividers. A sample curve snakes in and out of the network,
crossing 15 of the segments but leaving one uncrossed, so it is not a solution.]

The rules rule out tricks: the line may not pass through a vertex, run along a
segment, or rely on folding the paper.

On a flat plane the task is provably impossible. The questions to answer:
can it be done if the network is drawn on the surface of a **sphere**? What
about on the surface of a **torus** (a doughnut shape)?
