title: "The Twiddled Bolts"
---

Two identical bolts are laid side by side so that their helical thread grooves
mesh with one another. Hold each bolt by its head so that it cannot spin about
its own axis, and move the two around each other the way you'd "twiddle" your
thumbs, in the direction indicated in the picture.

[Figure: two identical threaded bolts lying parallel and offset, their threads
interlocked in the middle. The lower bolt's head is at the far left and the
upper bolt's head is at the far right, so the heads face away from each other.
Two curved arrows, one wrapped over the upper bolt and one under the lower bolt,
show the circular twiddling motion each bolt makes around the other.]

As you twiddle, do the two heads (a) move closer together, (b) move farther
apart, or (c) stay the same distance from each other? Work it out by reasoning,
not by trying it with real bolts.
