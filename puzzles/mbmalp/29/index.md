No one has found a formula for the number of ways to fold a road map along $n$
creases. A puzzle by Henry Ernest Dudeney hints at how hard the counting is.

Take a rectangular sheet divided into eight squares (two rows of four) and
number the squares on one face only, as below. There are 40 distinct ways to
fold the sheet along its creases into a single square packet with square 1 face
up on top and the rest underneath. The task: fold it so the squares end up in
order from 1 to 8 down the stack, with 1 on top.

[Figure: a 2×4 grid numbered, top row left to right: 1, 8, 7, 4; bottom row:
2, 3, 6, 5. Beside it, a small drawing of a folded packet with the "1" face up.]
