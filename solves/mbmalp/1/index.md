Yes, on any point of a circle centered on the South Pole with radius $r$ miles satisfying:

$$r = 1 + \frac{1}{2c\pi}$$

for $c \in \mathbb{N}$ (the natural numbers $1, 2, 3 \dots$)

ex. $r = 1 + \frac{1}{2\pi},\ 1 + \frac{1}{4\pi},\ 1 + \frac{1}{6\pi} \dots$

Because you walk 1 mile south and end up on a circle of radius $\frac{1}{2c\pi}$. The circumference of that circle is $2\pi\left(\frac{1}{2c\pi}\right) = \frac{1}{c}$, so $1$, or $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3} \dots$ Walking 1 mile east you will go around the pole $c$ times and end up where you started before walking east, then walk 1 mile north to where you began.
